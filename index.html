What we do

Program design

Daily structure, house expectations, peer roles and resident supports, built from recovery research and from what residents say they need. Welcoming, person-centered, and ready for people with co-occurring mental health needs.

Listening to people

Interviews and focus groups with residents, families, staff and referral partners. The kind of fieldwork Josh has done for more than a decade.

Referral networks

Mapping and strengthening ties in both directions: with the treatment programs, hospitals, courts, harm reduction services and recovery centers that send people your way, and with the jobs, schools, housing and care they move on to.

Knowing what works

Light, sustainable tracking of individual and program-level outcomes, during someone's stay and through each next step, with reports your staff can act on and funders and regulators expect to see.

Grant writing

Finding and writing applications for public, opioid settlement and foundation funding, including through nonprofit partners. Written by someone who has drafted federal NIH applications, with the evidence ready to back them up.

Getting started

Planning, positioning and partnerships for operators opening a new residence or adding services.

Two programs can follow the same rules and get very different results. The difference usually lives in how the place is run.

Built for New York

New York State's addiction services agency has set out its priorities through 2029 in its 2025–2029 Strategic Plan. Our work helps programs meet them.

Services people want and use

The plan asks for services that are desired, valued and utilized. We find out what residents and families actually want, and build it in.

Measured outcomes

The state is moving toward individual and program-level outcomes across the continuum. We help programs set up measures now, before they're required.

Co-occurring needs

With a new Co-Occurring Capable designation on the way, we help programs screen for and support mental health needs like depression and ADHD.

Young adults

The state is opening youth clubhouses and its first young adult recovery residence. Young adults have been the focus of Josh's research for years.

Housing and recovery after treatment

The plan commits to supportive housing for people leaving residential treatment. We help residences become the next step people are referred to.

Who we are

Josh Aleksanyan

Co-founder · PhD, Sociology

Josh is an organizational sociologist with 17 years of experience studying addiction and psychiatric services, especially for young adults, pregnant women and adults living with ADHD. He trained as an ethnographer and has spent years in treatment programs listening to the people who run them and the people who use them.

He has written federal NIH grant applications, helped design a funded National Institute on Drug Abuse research supplement, and worked with state agencies on statewide treatment data. He is currently helping build a recovery residence in New York and designing its peer-led program.

His research has appeared in Social Science & Medicine, Ethnography, Stigma and Health and leading addiction medicine journals. Born and raised in Brooklyn; studied sociology and dance at Bard.
